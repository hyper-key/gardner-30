Construction experiments, 2026-11-27

Literature and provenance

Donald Knuth reported a white-square n+1 upper bound for boards through n=30
in his own 26 August 2014 comment on https://oeis.org/A219760 . The numerical
sequence stops at n=29. Its 2024 extension to 28 and 29 is credited to Andy
Huchala. Huchala's posted Gurobi model is based on Rob Pratt's ILP and contains
no instance coordinates: https://oeis.org/A219760/a219760_1.py.txt .
John Schmitt's 2025 Linz slides show a question mark at n=30:
https://www.algebra.uni-linz.ac.at/Students/Seminare/s25/No3Talk_Extended.pdf .
Searches of these sources, the 2014 and 2025 papers, and Knuth's 2014
correspondence reproduced in Schmitt's 2019 talk did not locate the original
31 coordinates. The witness here independently reproduces the reported
construction class and bound. It is not attributed to Knuth as a transcription.

In Schmitt's reproduction of Knuth's correspondence, slides 25–30,
https://community.middlebury.edu/~jschmitt/talks/No3talk-Feb%2719.pdf ,
Knuth describes SAT, one-color and odd-row restrictions and further permutation
structure. Knuth reported that SAT found an 18-queen witness by searching at
most 19 but ran hours without finding one when asked for at most 18. His
12-queen equality exception had all eight board symmetries and used both colors.
The sequence data are reported outcomes; the computational claims
below have independently checkable saved inputs and controls. The proof in
Cooper–Pikhurko–Schmitt–Warrington, Theorem 1, supplies the even-board lower
bound; the sharpened result of Oh–Schmitt–Wang applies to n=1 mod 4.

Coordinate convention: [row,column], top left [0,0]. Empty squares can be
blocked only by a saturated row, column, difference or sum diagonal.

Experiments and decisions

C1. Could Knuth's reported 31-white-queen result be exhibited explicitly?
Prescribe two queens at even-even sites in each even row and each even
column, plus one additional odd-odd queen (2h+1,2k+1). Search is restricted
for construction. Any uncovered cell must be odd-odd. For (2i+1,2j+1),
the two compressed diagonal labels are i-j and i+j+1. Choose a cover by these
labels for all (i,j) except (h,k), and force those diagonal occupancies to two.
For each parity class enumerate possible unions U of sum-label neighborhoods
of unselected difference labels. Include U and all differences whose
neighborhoods are not subsets of U. Every cover contains one produced this way.
The even block was additionally made invariant under transposition as a search
heuristic. Every produced placement was checked on all 900 squares.

Initial fixed seed 148, one Z3 4.16.0 process at a time, 300 trials with
160ms each yielded no witness (9 unknown). Widening to 320ms and 1,600
planned trials returned a witness at zero-based trial 410 in 14.404s wall,
after 402 UNSAT and eight unknown. The template contents and logs are saved.
Fast replay uses profiles.py rather than repeating this stochastic-budget stage:
the first moments fix sum(d)=0 and sum(s)=2m(m-1), and the second moment is
sum(d*d+(s-m+1)^2)=2m(m^2-1)/3 for m=n/2. These follow by substituting
d=i-j and s=i+j and summing over the 2m even-block queens with each i and j
repeated twice. Enumerate residual diagonal occupancies respecting these
moments before PB solving. Seed 111, 300ms per solver query, 200 query ceiling:
at hole (5,0), 11th hole considered, 3rd query, witness in 0.298s.
All outputs refer to witness31-initial.json, identical to witness31.json.
Do not interpret reported local solver UNSATs as certificates.

C2. Are the size-n 10,18,26 constructions and the exceptional 12 construction
available for calibration? Remove the additional odd-odd queen, apply identical
diagonal profiles, then accept only actual witnesses. The 700ms per query
ceiling suffices; all three found on first query, within 0.05 seconds each.
Independent small direct code enumerates eightfold-orbit pairs on the 12 board
to reconstruct its witness. The five saved witnesses (including 31) all pass
controls.py's direct triple test and every single-square extension test.
Their optimality for n=10,12,18,26 also uses the published even-board bound.
None of these coordinates is a claimed transcription. The exact 13x13
compressed parity-subgrid profile enumeration produced 14 inclusion-minimal
covers surviving the 13+13 label budget and one distinct multiplicity profile
surviving the moments. See controls.log.

C3. Can an intact known 26 be extended using four queens of either color?
For each of the 25 translations within a 30x30 board, put down witness26.json
and enumerate every legal four-point addition by first addable square in
lexicographic order. Bit masks remove newly blocked squares after each
addition. All placements of the subgroup of the eight board isometries of
this seed are included up to the corresponding relative translation and an
isometry of the large board. The line multiplicity profile, together with
the fact that all seed cells lie on full rows, suffices to determine all
addable sets. The single-profile computation in C2 therefore extends these
observations to the other seeds with two queens in every even row and
every even column on an embedded 26x26 after symmetries. It does not cover
arbitrary 26 seeds. Sum over translations (counting each separately):
19,605,864 legal four-queen extensions. Best left 31 addable cells. Wall
22.601s, one process. Full histograms, best board and 4 extra points in
transplant-result.json. Direct enumeration reproduces all counts and
hole histograms for six small seed/depth pairs; independent triple checks
give the saved 30-board best exactly 31 legal additions. Pivot away from
unchanged seeds: large holes remain even under unrestricted-color additions.

C4. Does the mixed-color eightfold symmetry at n=12 suggest a 28-point
eightfold-invariant core + two arbitrary additions (total 30) at n=30?
On an even board eightfold orbits have sizes 8 or 4. A 4-orbit has two
points on each main diagonal, so at most one 4-orbit is legal. Hence
any 28-core has three distinct 8-orbits and one 4-orbit. Enumerate all
such legal cores. Given two added squares, the only newly saturated lines
are initially singleton lines and at most one formerly empty common line.
Their coverage plus the two squares bounds how many holes can be filled.
The algorithm prunes when this optimistic bound cannot beat the incumbent,
and otherwise scores the pair by checking every hole. With 268,750 legal
cores total, best remaining holes = 32. Independent point-triple enumeration
reproduced the best scores for 4-core+2 on the 6,8,10 boards (4,14,32).
Raw logs record the 0.98 CPU second and the best board. This restricted result
does not rule out non-invariant cores. The 28+2 and intact-26 routes are poor
starting points, so no larger search of either type was launched.

C5. Could regular row and column occupancy patterns evade the interval,
one-swap and monochrome obstructions? Enumerate every balanced length-30
mask with a period dividing 30 and at most ten; periods 2,6,10 suffice,
since a repeated odd-length block cannot have density 1/2. There are 270
masks. Reversing either axis independently by a board reflection preserves
the problem, yielding 135 representatives. Transposition exchanges occupied
axis sets, so checking every unordered pair with repetition gives 9,180
cases. No cyclic shifts are quotiented. For each case require exactly two
queens in each prescribed row and column: every queen then belongs to their
Cartesian product and its complementary product must be covered diagonally.
The experiment imports the earlier axis_matching.py screen without changing it.

Resource ceiling one CPU minute. Generation took 2.761 CPU seconds. A
matching of >30 different uncovered cells with pairwise distinct difference
and pairwise distinct sum labels rejects 9,108 cases. A cell lacking two
potential queens on either diagonal rejects 54, and forcing >15 saturated
lines of one diagonal slope rejects 17. Passing pair (77,77) is precisely
even rows and even columns. The closure-based necessary moment screen in
controls.py, independent of the other structural analysis, returns 16
minimal covers within the count budget and zero compatible profiles at
board side 30. Thus every axis pair in this periodic family is eliminated.
No mixed pattern, including anything resembling the n=12 axes, survived.
These are restricted certificate-backed computational results, not a claim
about all 30-queen placements.

Raw 9,179 rejection proofs: periodic-cert.json.gz (deterministic gzip,
400,282 bytes). check_periodic.py independently regenerates all mask pairs,
inverts diagonal labels to board coordinates, validates disjoint labels in
each matching and checks forced lines directly. The checker accepts the
original and rejects a corrupted matching. For the fastest replay from this
directory use “python3 periodic.py && python3 check_periodic.py”. For the
single surviving axis pair, controls.py additionally runs moment enumeration.

C6. Can a 30-queen maximal placement have exactly 15 paired difference
diagonals and 13 paired sum diagonals? This is a full diagonal-count subcase;
the seed, periodicity, and symmetry restrictions used earlier are absent.

Inputs from the coordinator's mathematical reduction: each of the 15 active
rows and columns has two queens. The 15 unused index sets on each axis have
a common span e and, after simultaneous reflection of both axes, each sums
to 225. A single-axis reflection exchanges complete and deficient diagonal
families when necessary. Let A and B be the unused index sets after translating
their minima to zero and identifying the endpoints 0 and e, giving size-14
sets in the cyclic group of order e containing zero. The unused product has
56 perimeter squares. With only 28 saturated diagonals, each must hit exactly
two perimeter squares, and no perimeter square can be hit twice. Therefore
each cell (a,b) of the unused grid lies either on a sum line having both
boundary intercepts among the corresponding unused axis values, or on a
difference line with the same property. Reading intercepts modulo e gives

    (a+b in A and a+b in B) or (a-b in A and b-a in B).

This is the necessary condition of Karandikar and Dutta, section 5.2,
Question 5.8, DOI 10.37236/12003. We do not use their conjectural symmetry or
any computed classification from their paper. The condition is necessary
irrespective of the relative sizes of the two diagonal families; its
application here uses exactly two hits for every saturated line.

Enumerate spans 14 through 29, transposing A and B to avoid duplication.
The full 15-element unused axis is t + (A union {e}), so its forced translation
is t=(225-e-sum(A))/15, an integer between 0 and 29-e. For b in B the
individual condition A subset ((A-b) union (A+b)) is necessary; reject an A
when fewer than 14 possible step values b remain. The primary implementation
modular_axes.cpp uses bit rotations. It tests the full original modular
condition against each possible B. Independent verify_modular_axes.cpp
instead generates increasing full-board 15-subsets of sum 225 using elementary
bounds on the remaining sum, and checks the step filter and pair condition
by scalar loops. Both emit the same 46 pairs, byte for byte (0.44 and 1.76
CPU seconds). The per-span counts in order e=14..29 are

    1, 0, 1, 3, 4, 3, 2, 3, 5, 3, 5, 0, 8, 0, 7, 1.

For 43 pairs modular-certificate.json exhibits 29 unused cells whose difference
labels are distinct and whose sum labels are distinct. Covering them requires
at least 29 saturated diagonals. One pair admits an unused cell with fewer
than two possible queens along each of its two diagonals. A further pair has
both active axes 7..21: targets (0,j), j=22..29, and (29,j), j=0..6 force
all 14 sum labels 22..35, too many. The remaining pair is exactly the
odd-index unused product, already excluded by the monochrome argument.
check_modular_certificate.py checks every stored obstruction and rejects a
corrupted matching (modular-cert-check.log).

A parallel mathematical check for these same 46 pairs is retained. Enumerate
all perfect matchings of 56 perimeter cells using diagonal edges that could
hold two active queens. There are 189,824 matchings over all pairs, 40,263
with a 15+13 split, and only seven cover the full unused product. All seven
come from the single monochrome axis pair. Their 15 paired difference keys D
include all 30 queens. Their 13 paired sum keys S account for 26, leaving four
distinct singleton sum keys. The whole-board first and second moments require

    sum(singletons)=2(sum(active rows)+sum(active columns)-sum(S)),
    sum(singletons squared)=4(sum(active rows squared)+sum(active columns squared))
                            -2 sum(d in D, d*d)-2 sum(s in S, s*s).

None of the seven admits such a quadruple. This enumeration used 2.56 CPU
seconds. All budget limits were below the assigned two core-minutes.

C7. Independent audit of the complete 15+14 projected domain.

With equal span e let the two unused axis sets include 0 and e after
translation. Reducing modulo e gives two size-14 sets U,V. Let h_i count
the distinct unused perimeter cells on selected physical diagonal i. At
most two such cells lie on one diagonal, including corner-touching lines.
Since 29 lines cover the entire perimeter, sum h_i >= 56. Therefore
sum(2-h_i) <= 2, even if cells have multiple covers. At most two lines
have h_i < 2. Every other line obeys the modular condition from C6.
A failed modular square can therefore only have been covered by one of
at most two exceptional *typed* modular labels. Assigning the residues
of physical line keys is a relaxation: physically distinct lines may share
one residue. The proof requires no converse. Cells with residue 0 may use
either actual endpoint; choose the point with smaller coordinate throughout.
Selected diagonal capacities always refer to ordinary board coordinates.

For one fixed axis U and opposite step y, defects are x in U for which
neither x+y nor x-y lies in U. A given exceptional diagonal removes at
most one distinct x in this residue column. For at least 14 y's the defects
must all be coverable by the same two typed labels. Our independent generator
tests each candidate first label, then counts how many further rows each
possible second label repairs. It enumerates fixed-density cyclic necklaces
and restores every rotation containing 0. A cyclic shift U -> U+t preserves
this unary condition: defective x shifts to x+t and both typed repair keys
shift by t. No cyclic symmetry restriction survives into the pair list.
Assertions verify that each generated necklace is minimal over its rotations,
that these representatives are strictly increasing, and that all their
distinct rotations with 0 present total exactly binomial(e-1,13) before any
filtering. These checks give an exhaustive domain independent of assumptions
about correctness of the necklace recursion.

For each located pair U,V, take all modularly failed cells and view them as
edges between difference and sum residues. A cover of size two can use two
difference keys, two sum keys, or one of each. The independent generator
tests each possibility directly, with an early three-disjoint-edge rejection.
Completely specified translations have equal row and column totals, hence
15*(t_V-t_U)=sum(U)-sum(V). This is the existing equal-active-sums identity
in the orientation with D complete. Only transpose is quotiented. No
mean-14-or-15 condition was used at 15+14.

C8. Independent audit of 15+15 with equal spans.

Here the two label budgets are separate, a fact needed because 30 diagonals
have four more potential perimeter hits than the 56 required. On the two
horizontal sides together there are 30 distinct targets. Only the two
main diagonals can touch both horizontal sides of the square frame.
Thus at most two selected lines miss both horizontal sides: with k missing
lines there are at most (30-k)+2 hits. Every U-only failed cell must be
covered by one of those lines, since a line that meets a horizontal side at
a target has its required x-intercept in U. The vertical pair of sides yields
a separate (possibly different) budget of two labels for the V-only failures.
Corner hits are counted once per side pair even when both a horizontal and
a vertical constraint govern that corner.

The unary shape enumeration from C7 therefore still applies. Each axis has
all translations restored and its full 30-bit mask is canonicalized by
reflection. There is no presupposed symmetry of the placement. Board
reflections and transposition exchange complete diagonal families and
preserve the split 15+15. Group the axes by 21(sum z)^2+5 sum z^2 on
the *unused* centered indices z=2i-29; this equals the previously derived
active invariant H plus a constant independent of the axes. On each axis
separately audit_full_frame.py uses augmenting-path maximum matching on the
bad-label graph to reject any matching of size three. By the bipartite
vertex-cover theorem a cover of size at most two exists exactly when no
three-edge matching exists. The independent result agrees as a set of
canonical actual axis pairs with all 11,691 primary pairs. The canonical
SHA-256 is 538819b8d1504c3aa69fca0a3d9b5db122a05f8e6e515230522e854f4423cf4b
(2.34 CPU seconds beyond shape generation). No conclusion about unequal
spans follows from the equal-span test alone.

Direct controls enumerate all original axis sets and all pairs of typed
exception residues on boards 6,8,10; both programs match the resulting
small projected domains. Independent physical-grid tests in frame_controls.py
compare modular predictions against actual diagonal contacts, including
1,288 lines with zero marked frame contacts, 5,180 with one and 1,862 with
two. No modular implication failed. See frame-audit-controls.log.
