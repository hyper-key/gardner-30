# Lean evidence

Use the toolchain, manifest, packages, external kernels and Z3 from the existing proofs project. In a fresh local clone of this branch on the provisioned machine, from its project root:

```sh
mkdir -p .lake
cp -a /home/dev/projects/proofs/.lake/packages .lake/
bash Gardner/formal/benchmark.sh
bash Gardner/formal/replay.sh
```

The optional timing baseline replays with `python3 Gardner/formal/calibrate.py`.

The copy is only needed if dependencies are absent. Lake rebuilds local theorem modules. The comparator requires its normal nested bubblewrap environment. The host's outer desktop sandbox initially blocked its network namespace (the loopback NETLINK_ROUTE socket). Running these same scripts with permission to launch bubblewrap allowed the verifier to complete. All comparator runs used its standard isolation. This filesystem does not support copy-on-write cloning, so the run used an ordinary dependency-cache copy.

## Scope of each result

* `GardnerDefs.lean` fixes a list representation of a finite set of squares. Validity requires no duplicate, both coordinates below the board size, and line occupancy at most two in all four directions through every entry. A line with no entries has occupancy zero. Nonnegative coordinates are enforced by the type. Goodness additionally requires that every absent on-board square gives an invalid list when prepended.
* `GardnerChallenge.lean` fixes the three public statements independently of witness data. `diff_key_iff` identifies equality of shifted difference keys with ordinary equality of the two integer-coordinate differences, expressed by cross addition. `add_fails_iff` derives the saturated-line test from literal insertion and proves the converse for any legal placement and vacant on-board square. `upper31` proves existence of a list of length 31 good on the 30×30 board.
* `GardnerSolution.lean` proves the public statements. A certificate lists lines with two queens. Lean checks the full list for validity, every listed line for occupancy two, and all 900 squares for listed-line coverage or occupancy. `good_of_lines` transfers these finite checks to literal maximality. The data in `GardnerData.lean` regenerate exactly from the JSON via `cert_data.py`. The Python script supplies constants and has no place in the proof trust chain.
* `controls.lean` checks an illegal triple in each family, duplicates, both forms of coordinate overflow, a valid nonmaximal placement, and a good placement with a queen sharing no line with another queen. These last two controls distinguish single-square maximality from two easy changes to the specification.
* `Perimeter.lean` proves `Gardner.Perimeter.lower30` directly for the identical `Good 30 q`. For a hypothetical list of length at most 29, each family has at most 14 saturated lines. Use the frame of the product of unsaturated rows and unsaturated columns. A diagonal has at most two frame points because its intersection injects into the two possible entry/exit sides. A frame point is occupied or belongs to a saturated diagonal. The proof counts the occupied exceptions outside saturated rows and outside saturated columns separately. The resulting two inequalities contradict the exact frame size. The independently compared lower target appears in `PerimeterChallenge.lean` and `lower.json`. `replay_lower.sh` runs it separately and is also called from `replay.sh`.
* `Bounds.lean` defines the attained least feasible cardinality with Nat.find, proves it belongs to {30,31}, and proves that it is 30 if and only if there is a legal maximal size-30 witness. See the lower comparator, perimeter, and bounds logs.
* `Moment.lean` proves the total squared difference plus centered-sum moment of 2240 on the compressed 15×15 grid assuming precisely two entries in every row and column (encoded by the projection multisets). `Arithmetic.lean` checks the final seven-target integer argument conditional on its coordinate bounds, sum, and permitted targets. The implication from board geometry to these hypotheses requires a separate argument.
* `GridFunctional.lean` proves that any integer polynomial represented by a finite list of monomials of total degree at most 59 which vanishes on the 900 points of the centered grid has zero coefficient of x^29 y^29. The explicit signed binomial weights satisfy their defining recurrence. The proof evaluates 31 one-dimensional moments and factors the monomial functional, then proves linearity for arbitrary term lists. It checks the signed-grid values of all ten homogeneous degree-58 quota products directly, and proves that the bounded even singleton-pair sum must vanish if it satisfies the stated linear equation. These are unconditional algebraic implications, whose applications to line covers require the geometric argument.
* `logs/axioms.txt`, `logs/moment.txt` and `logs/arithmetic.txt` give axiom reports. The three public comparator statements, and the two separate structural theorems, use only propext, Classical.choice, and Quot.sound. Kernel-reduced `decide` discharges finite facts. Challenge and deliberate incomplete control files contain placeholders. The formal proof establishes the bounds 30 and 31.

## Experiments and decisions

`StructureBridge.lean` and `AffineProducts.lean` connect the actual full-line keys of a board to the centered-grid polynomial, prove intercept independence for the degree-58 test, and prove general one- and two-omission recurrences. The kernel theorem `Gardner.Bridge.coarse_saturation` assumes a good size-30 placement with no isolated queens, and yields the pair-count restrictions. The grid audit includes `FullMoment.lean`. The remaining links include arbitrary slot assignment for isolated queens, specialization of the higher-degree intercept expansions, and completeness of the projection and graph reductions. These are supplied in the written proof and replayable enumeration.

`Gardner/exact/Marginal.lean` proves the residual-moment obstruction from paired axes. The separate `Gardner/exact/LocalCertificates.lean` is an executable validating each local record against the enumerated domains. Its run and negative controls are part of the top-level replay.
